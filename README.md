## Introduction

Applied linguistics investigates real-world problems where language plays a central role. Rather than treating language solely as an abstract formal system, the discipline connects linguistic theory to practical human activity — examining how languages are learned and taught, how the mind produces and comprehends speech, and how social context shapes communication.

This curriculum assumes no prior background in linguistics or language science. It is designed for self-directed learners — including current or aspiring language teachers, curriculum designers, and independent students of language — who want a structured, academically grounded foundation in the field rather than ad-hoc tips or disconnected overviews.

### How to Navigate the Sequence

The curriculum moves deliberately from descriptive linguistic foundations to applied domains:

- **Linguistic foundations first:** Begin with Introduction to Linguistics to build a broad conceptual map of the discipline. From there, work through Phonetics and Phonology, Morphology, Syntax, and Grammar, and Semantics and Pragmatics. These foundational subjects provide the descriptive tools, transcription skills, and structural concepts you will need to analyze language systematically. The open-access textbook *Essentials of Linguistics* appears across several of these subjects and serves as a dependable, unified backbone throughout this early stage.
- **Applied domains next:** Once you understand how language systems are organized, proceed into the applied and cognitive subjects: Sociolinguistics, Second Language Acquisition, Language Teaching Methodology, and Psycholinguistics and First Language Acquisition. While the early structural subjects build directly upon each other and are best studied in sequence, the applied subjects offer greater flexibility. You can adjust the order among them to suit your immediate goals — for instance, prioritizing Second Language Acquisition and Language Teaching Methodology if your primary aim is classroom practice.

### Scope

This curriculum covers only the foundational core — the baseline knowledge that practitioners across applied linguistics share. It does not attempt to cover every specialized niche or advanced debate. Once you have worked through these core subjects, you can expand your studies by visiting [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md) to explore specialized subfields or working through [Projects](projects.md) to test your understanding with hands-on analysis and applied tasks.

## Applied Linguistics

The following constitutes the foundational core for self-directed learners in Applied Linguistics. Each subject represents knowledge that virtually every practitioner in the field needs before any specialization. The sequence moves from general linguistic foundations to applied domains.

### Introduction to Linguistics

A survey of the scientific study of language, covering its major subfields and core concepts that underpin all later work in applied linguistics.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd ed. (Anderson, Bjorkman, Denis, et al.)](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/) - a free, open-access textbook with short accompanying videos. Its chapters on phonetics, phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, sociolinguistics, and psycholinguistics make it a self-contained backbone for the entire core sequence below.

[MIT 24.900 Introduction to Linguistics, Spring 2022 (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://archive.org/details/MIT24.900S22) - full video recordings of the MIT course, useful if you prefer learning from structured lectures. The Essentials of Linguistics textbook works well alongside these lectures, and the two together form a strong foundation.

[Crash Course Linguistics (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLmsIjFudc1l1BwLOh-O64hcjQnuKzkIU2) - a 16-episode animated series that offers an engaging first pass through the entire field. Watch this first to build a mental map, then use the textbook or MIT lectures for depth.

[Miracles of Human Language: An Introduction to Linguistics (Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/human-language) - an audit-mode course from the Meertens Institute and University of Amsterdam featuring interviews with working linguists. A structured alternative if you prefer a guided course format over a standalone textbook.

### Phonetics and Phonology

The physical production and perception of speech sounds, and how languages organize those sounds into systems.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd ed. (eCampusOntario)](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/) - its phonetics and phonology chapters provide the core terminology and analytical framework. Use this as your primary structured reading.

[Sounds of Speech (University of Iowa)](https://soundsofspeech.uiowa.edu/) - an interactive animated reference showing how each English, Spanish, and German speech sound is articulated. Use it alongside the phonetics chapter to connect the IPA symbols to actual vocal tract anatomy.

[The Virtual Linguistics Campus - VLC105 Phonetics, Phonology & Transcription (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLDDGPdw7e6AjA12Fj3fXCSo_gCgR6kGSE) - search "VLC105 Phonetics, Phonology & Transcription" on YouTube to find this playlist from the Virtual Linguistics and Literature Campus channel. It offers systematic e-lectures on transcription and phonological analysis, providing a more structured audiovisual companion to the textbook.

### Morphology, Syntax, and Grammar

The internal structure of words and sentences, and how languages build meaning from smaller grammatical units.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd ed. (eCampusOntario)](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/) - its morphology and syntax chapters cover word formation, tree structures, and basic syntactic analysis. Start here for a free, clear introduction.

[The Study of Language, 7th ed. (George Yule, Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781108730713) - a widely used introductory textbook that treats morphology and syntax with more extended examples and exercises than most open resources. Use it as a paid supplement if you find you want more worked examples than the open textbook provides.

[Language Files: Materials for an Introduction to Language and Linguistics, 12th ed. (Ohio State University Department of Linguistics)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780814252703) - the classic reference-style textbook used in many university intro courses. Its morphology and syntax sections are especially thorough. Consult it selectively rather than reading cover-to-cover.

### Semantics and Pragmatics

How languages encode meaning, and how context and speaker intention shape interpretation.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd ed. (eCampusOntario)](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/) - its semantics and pragmatics chapters cover lexical meaning, compositional semantics, speech acts, and implicature. This is the free starting point.

[Semantics, 4th ed. (John I. Saeed, Wiley-Blackwell)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781119704045) - a standard university textbook on semantics that goes deeper into formal approaches, sense relations, and cognitive semantics. Use it after the open textbook chapter if you want a more rigorous treatment.

[An Introduction to English Semantics and Pragmatics (Edinburgh University Press)](https://edinburghuniversitypress.com/book/an-introduction-to-english-semantics-and-pragmatics) - a focused treatment of how semantics and pragmatics interact in English, useful for applied linguists working with English specifically. Its clear structure makes it a good companion rather than a substitute for the broader semantics text.

### Sociolinguistics

How social factors - class, gender, ethnicity, region, identity - shape language use and variation.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd ed. (eCampusOntario)](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/) - its sociolinguistics chapter provides a solid introduction to language variation, dialects, and language attitudes. Start here for free.

[An Introduction to Sociolinguistics, 7th ed. (Ronald Wardhaugh and Janet M. Fuller, Wiley-Blackwell)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781118732298) - the standard survey textbook, covering language variation, multilingualism, language planning, and language and identity in far greater depth. This is the one book in the core worth purchasing if you can only afford a few.

[The Routledge Handbook of Sociolinguistics Around the World (Routledge)](https://www.routledge.com/Routledge-Handbook-of-Sociolinguistics-Around-the-World/Ball/book/hb/9780415874277) - search "Routledge Handbook of Sociolinguistics Around the World" for this edited collection. It broadens the perspective beyond English and North America, which most introductory textbooks still center. Consult individual chapters rather than reading straight through.

### Second Language Acquisition

The study of how people learn languages beyond their first, covering learner language, interlanguage, individual differences, and learning contexts.

[How Languages Are Learned, 4th ed. (Patsy M. Lightbown and Nina Spada, Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780194541268) - the single most accessible introduction to SLA research, written for teachers rather than researchers. This is the essential book for this subject.

[Introducing Second Language Acquisition, 3rd ed. (Muriel Saville-Troike, Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781316603925) - a more academic treatment that takes a linguistic, psychological, and social approach to SLA. Use it either as an alternative to Lightbown & Spada if you prefer a more theoretical framing, or as a follow-up for more depth.

[The Study of Second Language Acquisition, 2nd ed. (Rod Ellis, Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780194422514) - Ellis's comprehensive reference work on SLA. Consult it selectively on specific topics rather than reading cover-to-cover; it functions as an encyclopedia of the field.

### Language Teaching Methodology

The practical and theoretical approaches to teaching languages, including historical methods, communicative language teaching, and current task-based and content-based approaches.

[Approaches and Methods in Language Teaching, 3rd ed. (Jack C. Richards and Theodore S. Rodgers, Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781107675964) - the definitive survey of language teaching methods from Grammar Translation to Communicative Language Teaching and beyond. Essential reading for understanding why methods evolved and what assumptions each rests on.

[The Practice of English Language Teaching, 5th ed. (Jeremy Harmer, Pearson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781447980254) - a practical guide that bridges theory and classroom application, covering lesson planning, classroom management, and teaching specific skills. Use it alongside Richards & Rodgers for the practical dimension.

[TESOL 101: Free TESOL Course (OnTESOL)](https://ontesol.com/tesol-101/) - a free introductory TESOL course covering teaching fundamentals. Useful as a structured, self-paced starting point before diving into the more comprehensive textbooks.

### Psycholinguistics and First Language Acquisition

How humans process, produce, and acquire language cognitively, covering comprehension, production, and the developmental stages of first language learning.

[Essentials of Linguistics, 2nd ed. (eCampusOntario)](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/essentialsoflinguistics2/) - its psycholinguistics and language acquisition chapters provide a concise free introduction. Start here to get the core concepts.

[An Introduction to Psycholinguistics, 2nd ed. (Danny D. Steinberg and Natalia V. Sciarini, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781317895205) - a widely used textbook covering the psychology of language from learning to mind and brain. It bridges linguistics and psychology in a way that is directly relevant to applied linguistics.

[MIT 24.904J Language Acquisition (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/course/24-904j-language-acquisition-spring-2005/) - an MIT course covering first language acquisition, focusing on the development of linguistic structure. Search "24.904J Language Acquisition" on MIT OCW. The lecture notes and assignments are freely available and provide a structured university-level introduction.
