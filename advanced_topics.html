Advanced Topics: Applied Linguistics

These specialization tracks build directly upon the foundational knowledge established in the Core Curriculum, allowing self-directed learners to pursue advanced methodologies, practical frameworks, and specialized subdisciplines. Learners are encouraged to select one or two tracks aligned with their intellectual interests or professional goals rather than attempting to complete every track.

  • Language Assessment and Testing - The theoretical frameworks, statistical validity arguments, and practical design principles used to build, evaluate, and critically audit language tests.
  • Corpus Linguistics - Computational and empirical methods for querying, tagging, and analyzing large digital collections of authentic text and speech.
  • Advanced Discourse Analysis - Methodologies for analyzing language beyond the sentence, including critical discourse analysis, conversation analysis, multimodal semiotics, and discursive psychology.
  • English for Specific Purposes - The analysis, curriculum design, and pedagogical delivery of language instruction tailored to targeted academic, professional, and workplace contexts.
  • Intercultural Pragmatics and Communication - The study of how interlocutors from diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds negotiate meaning, manage interpersonal rapport, and realize speech acts.

Language Assessment and Testing

This track investigates how language ability is conceptualized, operationalized, and evaluated across educational, institutional, and societal contexts.

Test Justification and Validity Argumentation: Language Assessment in Practice (Lyle F. Bachman & Adrian S. Palmer, Oxford University Press) - introduces the Assessment Use Argument (AUA) framework, establishing how to articulate and justify the validity claims linking test performance to real-world educational decisions.

Classroom-Based Assessment Design: Language Assessment: Principles and Classroom Practices, 3rd ed. (H. Douglas Brown & Priyanvada Abeywickrama, Pearson) - a practical guide detailing how to construct, evaluate, and score authentic classroom assessment tasks across listening, speaking, reading, and writing.

Assessment Foundations and Practical Test Construction: Introduction to Language Testing and Assessment (Glenn Fulcher, YouTube) - a university-level video lecture series that systematically explores test specifications, rating scales, reliability, and validity theory.

Critical Language Testing and Social Policy: The Power of Tests: A Critical Perspective on the Uses of Language Tests (Elana Shohamy, Routledge) - examines how standardized language assessments function as instruments of power, social control, and immigration policy, training learners to conduct critical testing audits.

Corpus Linguistics

This track covers computational techniques and methodological workflows for analyzing electronic text databases to discover empirical patterns in vocabulary, grammar, and register.

Methodological Foundations and Corpus Theory: Corpus Linguistics: Method, Theory and Practice (Tony McEnery & Andrew Hardie, Cambridge University Press) - outlines the core quantitative methodologies of corpus research while demonstrating how empirical corpus evidence challenges and enriches broader linguistic theory.

Hands-On Corpus Building and Text Processing: Corpus Linguistics and New Technologies: Data, Language and Society (Lancaster University, edX) - an 8-week audit-mode course teaching corpus construction, tokenization, part-of-speech tagging, and data visualization.

Computer-Aided Text Analysis and Concordancing: AntConc Tutorial Series (Laurence Anthony, YouTube) - a guided tutorial series demonstrating practical workflows for keyword-in-context (KWIC) concordancing, collocation extraction, and n-gram analysis using the field's standard freeware toolkit.

Register Variation and Multi-Dimensional Analysis: Register, Genre, and Style, 2nd ed. (Douglas Biber & Susan Conrad, Cambridge University Press) - provides a comprehensive analytical framework for investigating how situational context and communicative purpose govern linguistic variation across spoken, written, and digital registers.

Corpus-Assisted Discourse Studies: Using Corpora in Discourse Analysis, 2nd ed. (Paul Baker, Bloomsbury Academic) - bridges corpus methodologies and qualitative discourse analysis, showing how frequency, keyness, and collocation uncover latent ideological stances in text collections.

Advanced Discourse Analysis

This track provides specialized methodological frameworks to analyze naturally occurring interaction, systemic power relations in text, and multimodal communication.

Critical Discourse Analysis: Analysing Discourse: Textual Analysis for Social Research (Norman Fairclough, Routledge) - provides a systematic three-dimensional framework combining linguistic textual analysis with social theory to reveal how power and ideology are reproduced in public discourse.

Conversation Analysis: An Introduction to Conversation Analysis (Charles Antaki, Loughborough University) - an interactive, audio-supported online tutorial that teaches Jeffersonian transcription notation and the structural mechanics of turn-taking, sequence organization, and repair.

Conversation Analysis: Conversation Analysis: An Introduction (Jack Sidnell, Wiley-Blackwell) - a comprehensive companion text to Antaki's online tutorial that examines the structural rules governing natural human conversation across institutional and everyday settings.

Multimodal Discourse Analysis: Reading Images: The Grammar of Visual Design, 3rd ed. (Gunther Kress & Theo van Leeuwen, Routledge) - the seminal work that extends systemic functional linguistics into visual semiotics, analyzing how layout, composition, and color construct meaning in multimodal texts.

Discursive Psychology: Discourse and Social Psychology: Beyond Attitudes and Behaviour (Jonathan Potter & Margaret Wetherell, SAGE Publications) - establishes how psychological categories such as memories, identities, and attitudes are actively constructed and negotiated in interaction rather than existing as static mental states.

English for Specific Purposes

This track explores how to design, analyze, and teach language courses targeted to specific academic disciplines, technical professions, and workplace environments.

ESP Foundations and Needs Analysis: Introducing English for Specific Purposes (Laurence Anthony, Routledge) - an accessible entry point covering target needs assessment, curriculum development, and materials adaptation for specialized learner groups.

Genre Analysis and Move Structures: Genre Analysis: English in Academic and Research Settings (John M. Swales, Cambridge University Press) - the canonical monograph that introduced move analysis and the Create-A-Research-Space (CARS) model for deconstructing academic and research writing.

ESP Course Development and Case Studies: Developing Courses in English for Specific Purposes (Helen Basturkmen, Palgrave Macmillan) - combines course design principles with practical case studies across medicine, business, engineering, and vocational trades.

Professional Communication in Practice: Language in Professional Life (The Open University / OpenLearn) - a free, structured course analyzing authentic spoken and written discourse across professional domains, including branding, corporate mediation, and healthcare communication.

Intercultural Pragmatics and Communication

This track explores how communication functions across cultural and linguistic boundaries, focusing on intercultural speech acts, politeness norms, and communicative misunderstandings.

Intercultural Communication and Negotiation: Intercultural Communication and Conflict Resolution (University of California, Irvine / Coursera) - a free-to-audit video course providing practical frameworks to diagnose intercultural misunderstandings and mediate communicative breakdowns.

Empirical Speech Act Realization: Pragmatics and Speech Acts (CARLA, University of Minnesota) - an open research repository providing detailed descriptions, empirical data, and pedagogical strategies for analyzing cross-cultural variations in apologies, requests, compliments, and refusals.

Rapport Management and Politeness Theory: Culturally Speaking: Culture, Communication and Politeness Theory, 2nd ed. (Helen Spencer-Oatey, Ed., Continuum) - presents the rapport management framework to explain how cultural values shape face considerations, social rights, and interactional goals during intercultural encounters.

Theoretical Foundations of Intercultural Pragmatics: Intercultural Pragmatics (Istvan Kecskes, Oxford University Press) - introduces the socio-cognitive model of intercultural pragmatics, analyzing how interlocutors without shared cultural backgrounds co-construct meaning and common ground in real time.
