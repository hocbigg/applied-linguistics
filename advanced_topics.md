# Advanced Topics: Applied Linguistics

These tracks build on the Core Curriculum and let self-directed learners go deeper into specialized methods, frameworks, and subfields. Choose one or two that fit your interests or professional goals rather than trying to complete all five.

- Language Assessment and Testing: the theory, validity arguments, and design principles used to build, evaluate, and audit language tests.
- Corpus Linguistics: computational and empirical methods for querying, tagging, and analyzing large digital collections of authentic text and speech.
- Advanced Discourse Analysis: methods for analyzing language beyond the sentence, including critical discourse analysis, conversation analysis, multimodal semiotics, and discursive psychology.
- English for Specific Purposes: analyzing, designing, and teaching language instruction for particular academic, professional, and workplace contexts.
- Intercultural Pragmatics and Communication: how speakers from different linguistic and cultural backgrounds negotiate meaning, manage rapport, and perform speech acts.

## Language Assessment and Testing

This track looks at how language ability is defined, measured, and evaluated in educational, institutional, and social contexts.

Test justification and validity argumentation: [Language Assessment in Practice (Lyle F. Bachman & Adrian S. Palmer, Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780194422932): introduces the Assessment Use Argument (AUA) framework, which shows how to state and justify the validity claims that link test performance to real-world educational decisions.

Classroom-based assessment design: [Language Assessment: Principles and Classroom Practices, 3rd ed. (H. Douglas Brown & Priyanvada Abeywickrama, Pearson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780134860220): a practical guide to constructing, evaluating, and scoring authentic classroom assessment tasks for listening, speaking, reading, and writing.

Assessment foundations and practical test construction: [Introduction to Language Testing and Assessment (Glenn Fulcher, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@glennfulcher5625): a university-level video lecture series on test specifications, rating scales, reliability, and validity theory.

Critical language testing and social policy: [The Power of Tests: A Critical Perspective on the Uses of Language Tests (Elana Shohamy, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780582423350): examines how standardized language tests act as instruments of power, social control, and immigration policy, and trains you to conduct critical testing audits.

## Corpus Linguistics

This track covers the computational techniques and workflows used to analyze electronic text databases and find empirical patterns in vocabulary, grammar, and register.

Methodological foundations and corpus theory: [Corpus Linguistics: Method, Theory and Practice (Tony McEnery & Andrew Hardie, Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521547369): outlines the core quantitative methods of corpus research and shows how corpus evidence challenges and enriches linguistic theory.

Hands-on corpus building and text processing: [Corpus Linguistics and New Technologies: Data, Language and Society (Lancaster University, edX)](https://www.edx.org/learn/social-sciences/lancaster-university-corpus-linguistics-and-new-technologies-data-language-society): an 8-week course, available in audit mode, that teaches corpus construction, tokenization, part-of-speech tagging, and data visualization.

Computer-aided text analysis and concordancing: [AntConc Tutorial Series (Laurence Anthony, YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLiRIDpYmiC0R3Vv5NncOuIqaUcyLLW7Ae): a guided tutorial series on keyword-in-context (KWIC) concordancing, collocation extraction, and n-gram analysis with AntConc, the field's standard freeware toolkit.

Register variation and multi-dimensional analysis: [Register, Genre, and Style, 2nd ed. (Douglas Biber & Susan Conrad, Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781108444088): a full analytical framework for investigating how situational context and communicative purpose shape linguistic variation across spoken, written, and digital registers.

Corpus-assisted discourse studies: [Using Corpora in Discourse Analysis, 2nd ed. (Paul Baker, Bloomsbury Academic)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781350083752): connects corpus methods to qualitative discourse analysis and shows how frequency, keyness, and collocation can reveal ideological stances hidden in text collections.

## Advanced Discourse Analysis

This track gives you specialized methods for analyzing naturally occurring interaction, power relations in text, and multimodal communication.

Critical discourse analysis: [Analysing Discourse: Textual Analysis for Social Research (Norman Fairclough, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780415258937): a systematic three-dimensional framework that combines linguistic textual analysis with social theory to show how power and ideology are reproduced in public discourse.

Conversation analysis: [An Introduction to Conversation Analysis (Charles Antaki, Loughborough University)](https://learn.lboro.ac.uk/ludata/cx/ca-tutorials/): an interactive online tutorial with audio that teaches Jeffersonian transcription notation and the mechanics of turn-taking, sequence organization, and repair.

Conversation analysis: [Conversation Analysis: An Introduction (Jack Sidnell, Wiley-Blackwell)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781405159012): a companion text to Antaki's tutorial that examines the rules governing natural conversation in institutional and everyday settings.

Multimodal discourse analysis: [Reading Images: The Grammar of Visual Design, 3rd ed. (Gunther Kress & Theo van Leeuwen, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780415672573): the key work extending systemic functional linguistics to visual semiotics, analyzing how layout, composition, and color construct meaning in multimodal texts.

Discursive psychology: [Discourse and Social Psychology: Beyond Attitudes and Behaviour (Jonathan Potter & Margaret Wetherell, SAGE Publications)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780803980563): argues that psychological categories such as memories, identities, and attitudes are actively constructed and negotiated in interaction, not fixed mental states.

## English for Specific Purposes

This track covers how to design, analyze, and teach language courses for particular academic disciplines, technical professions, and workplaces.

ESP foundations and needs analysis: [Introducing English for Specific Purposes (Laurence Anthony, Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138237957): an accessible entry point to target needs assessment, curriculum development, and materials adaptation for specialized learner groups.

Genre analysis and move structures: [Genre Analysis: English in Academic and Research Settings (John M. Swales, Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521338134): the monograph that introduced move analysis and the Create-A-Research-Space (CARS) model for breaking down academic and research writing.

ESP course development and case studies: [Developing Courses in English for Specific Purposes (Helen Basturkmen, Palgrave Macmillan)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780230227989): pairs course design principles with practical case studies from medicine, business, engineering, and vocational trades.

Professional communication in practice: [Language in Professional Life (The Open University / OpenLearn)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/languages/english-language/language-professional-life/content-section-0): a free, structured course that analyzes authentic spoken and written discourse in professional domains, including branding, corporate mediation, and healthcare communication.

## Intercultural Pragmatics and Communication

This track looks at how communication works across cultural and linguistic boundaries, with a focus on intercultural speech acts, politeness norms, and misunderstandings.

Intercultural communication and negotiation: [Intercultural Communication and Conflict Resolution (University of California, Irvine / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/intercultural-communication): a video course, free to audit, with practical frameworks for diagnosing intercultural misunderstandings and mediating communication breakdowns.

Empirical speech act realization: [Pragmatics and Speech Acts (CARLA, University of Minnesota)](https://archive.carla.umn.edu/speechacts/): an open research repository with detailed descriptions, empirical data, and teaching strategies for analyzing cross-cultural variation in apologies, requests, compliments, and refusals.

Rapport management and politeness theory: [Culturally Speaking: Culture, Communication and Politeness Theory, 2nd ed. (Helen Spencer-Oatey, Ed., Continuum)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781441189400): presents the rapport management framework, which explains how cultural values shape face considerations, social rights, and interactional goals in intercultural encounters.

Theoretical foundations of intercultural pragmatics: [Intercultural Pragmatics (Istvan Kecskes, Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199892655): introduces the socio-cognitive model of intercultural pragmatics and analyzes how speakers without a shared cultural background build meaning and common ground in real time.
